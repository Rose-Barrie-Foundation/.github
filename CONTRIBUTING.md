# Contributing

Thanks for considering it. This file applies across all Rose-Barrie Foundation repositories.

---

## Before you start

**Open an issue first** for anything substantial. It saves you building something we've already tried, or something that conflicts with a design decision made elsewhere.

Small fixes don't need this. Open the pull request.

## Licences

Each repository states its own licence. In general:

| Content | Licence |
|---|---|
| Hardware design files | `CERN-OHL-S-2.0` |
| Firmware and software | `GPL-3.0-or-later` |
| Documentation and data | `CC-BY-SA-4.0` |

Every repository states the licence **and its version** explicitly. If you're unsure which applies to a file, check the `LICENSE` file at the repository root and the SPDX header in the file itself.

The Rose-Barrie name, logos and design marks are **not** covered by these licences. They are trade-marks used under licence and are not free for reuse.

## The Contributor Licence Agreement

Before we can accept a substantial contribution, we ask you to sign a CLA. A bot will prompt you on your first pull request — it's one comment, and it remembers you afterwards.

**You do not need to sign** for a contribution that is only a typo fix, a formatting change, or a change of fewer than 15 lines that adds no new functionality or design.

### What it does

- **You keep the copyright** in everything you write. The CLA grants a licence; it does not transfer ownership.
- You grant us the right to publish your contribution, and to sell commercial licences to companies that want to build closed products on this work.
- **We commit to keep publishing your contribution openly.** If we ever stop, that commercial right comes back to you.
- It includes a patent licence covering your contribution, and that licence terminates for anyone who sues over the project.

## Why we ask for this

### Do I give up my copyright?

No. You keep it. You are granting a licence, not transferring ownership, and you can continue to use your own work however you like.

### Why does the Foundation need the right to sublicense?

The Foundation is not a registered charity. It cannot issue tax receipts and it is not eligible for most grant funding. Selling commercial licences — letting a company use the designs without publishing their own changes — is one of the few ways it can pay for research. That requires the ability to grant terms other than the open licence.

The Foundation is a non-profit society. It cannot distribute income to its members, and anything left on dissolution must go to another non-profit.

### Could you take the project closed?

No. The CLA commits us to keep publishing every accepted contribution under an open licence. That commitment also sits in the Foundation's bylaws, which are entrenched and cannot be changed by ordinary resolution.

And the work already published under `CERN-OHL-S-2.0` and `GPL-3.0-or-later` is irrevocable. Anyone can fork it, permanently, whatever happens to the Foundation.

### What happens if you break that promise?

If the Foundation stops publishing your contribution openly for a year, you can give notice. If it doesn't fix that within ninety days, the commercial sublicensing right over your work ends.

Licences already granted to other people are not disturbed — otherwise nobody could safely rely on a licence from the Foundation.

### Does reversion give the project to the contributors?

No, and it's worth being clear about that. Rights revert to each contributor over their own contributions only. There is no transfer of the project or of stewardship. What reversion does is make closing the project pointless rather than change who runs it.

### What if I contribute as part of my job?

Check whether your employer owns what you write. If they do, you need their permission or a waiver before submitting.

### Can I use AI tools?

Yes. We use them too — see our AI Use Statement.

We ask two things, and both are in the CLA: that you directed and shaped the result rather than submitting a tool's output unchanged, and that you have the rights you are granting us. We don't ask which tools you used.

### Is there a patent trap here?

The patent grant is the standard Apache-style one, narrowed to claims necessarily infringed by your own contribution. It also terminates for anyone who sues over the project.

The Foundation does not file patents on work it publishes.

## Making a good pull request

- **One thing per pull request.** Easier to review, easier to revert.
- **Explain the why**, not just the what. The commit history is the design record for this project, and it stays public permanently.
- **Include test results** where the change affects behaviour — measurements, captures, bench conditions.
- **Don't rewrite published history.** We don't force-push released work, and neither should a PR.

For hardware changes, include the source files in the preferred form for modification — not just exported outputs.

## Reporting a security issue

Do not open a public issue. See `SECURITY.md` for how to report privately.

---

## Reporting a privacy concern

If you believe something in this project collects, transmits or exposes personal data, tell us. Open an issue if it's a design question, or report it privately as a security issue if it's a live exposure.

The Foundation holds no identifiable personal data and does not publish it. If you think a design breaks that, we want to know before anyone builds it.

## Questions

Open an issue. Questions before signing the CLA are welcome — we'd rather answer them than have you sign something you're unsure about.
